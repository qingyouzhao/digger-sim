# Concept Art Brief — EXP-01: Classic Crawler
**Concept Art Director | Kids' Digger Simulator**

---

## World Context

The Classic Crawler is the workhorse of the Gasco Construction universe — a heavy tracked excavator manufactured by **Gasco Heavy Works**, the fictional universe's equivalent of Caterpillar or JCB. It rolls off an assembly line in a vast mid-century industrial plant somewhere in the Midwest, painted in the company's signature high-visibility yellow before being shipped to every building site, mine, and disaster zone on the map.

**Who made it:** A large, unionised manufacturing company with 70 years of heritage. Their designers believe in honesty: every visible part earns its place. No cosmetic covers, no aesthetic pretense. The exhaust stack is real, the teeth on the bucket are real, the grease nipples are real.

**For what purpose:** This is the machine kids already know. It lives in their picture books, on their bedroom wallpaper, in their toy box. It is the Platonic ideal of a digger. The design goal is not to invent something new — it is to make the most *archetypal* version of what a child pictures when they say "digger."

**Relationship to user (child player):** Power and safety in the same form. Big enough to be impressive, rounded enough not to feel threatening. The cab is a window into the driver's world — the child is meant to imagine themselves inside it.

**Real-world visual adjacencies:**
1. Caterpillar 320 / JCB JS220 — the proportional bible
2. Tonka pressed-steel toys from the 1970s — the nostalgic warmth
3. Bob the Builder (original CGI series) — the friendly-but-real balance point

**Emotional tone:** Competent. Cheerful. Inevitable. This machine has been at every construction site in the world and will be at the next one too.

---

## Thumbnail Directions

### Direction A — "The Tonka Classic"
One-sentence premise: Proportions taken from pressed-steel toy cars — squat, wide, impossibly stable-looking.
Silhouette: Very wide track footprint, low centre of gravity, boxy cab with single large windshield, boom arm angles forward aggressively.
Material one-liner: Stamped steel with visible seams and rivets, rubber track pads.
Mood word: **Nostalgic**

### Direction B — "The Real-Site Replica"
One-sentence premise: Scaled-down photorealism — every bolt, every hydraulic line, exactly as it appears on a working site.
Silhouette: Precise JCB proportions, counterweight block clearly readable from rear, angled cab glass, articulated boom.
Material one-liner: Industrial enamel paint, cast iron, hydraulic hose bundles.
Mood word: **Authentic**

### Direction C — "The Picture-Book Giant"
One-sentence premise: Children's illustration geometry — exaggerated proportions that communicate power and friendliness simultaneously, like a Pixar vehicle.
Silhouette: Oversized bucket, tiny cab with huge eyes-as-windows, chubby track rollers, thick heroic boom arm.
Material one-liner: Chunky moulded ABS in bright yellow, smooth surfaces with minimal surface detail.
Mood word: **Heroic**

---

## Primary Geometric Vocabulary

The selected direction blends A and C: toy-like proportions with enough industrial vocabulary to be instantly recognisable.

- **Primary shape:** Extruded rectangles with chamfered edges — boxes that feel solid, not sharp.
- **Boom and stick:** Tapered rectangular sections, wider at the pivot, narrowing toward the bucket — communicating stress distribution.
- **Cab:** A trapezoidal greenhouse — wider at the bottom, narrowing to roof. Front glass raked forward slightly.
- **Undercarriage:** Low and wide — track frames extend beyond the body width, giving a planted stance.
- **Bucket:** Curved back, straight spine along the cutting edge, large teeth projecting forward. The most recognisable element.
- **Counterweight:** Rounded horizontal slab at the rear — the visual anchor that balances the boom.

Avoid: thin sections, acute angles, delicate details. Every element reads from 20 metres away.

---

## Color Strategy

**Primary body:** Construction Yellow — warm, high-visibility, universally understood.
- Value: High brightness, medium saturation. Not neon, not pastel.
- Reference: Caterpillar Safety Yellow (#FFC200 in sRGB)

**Secondary accent:** Black — cab interior, track rubber, shadow pockets.
- Creates value contrast that reads the silhouette.

**Detail accent:** Chrome/polished silver — cylinder rods, pin joints.
- Communicates mechanical precision without detracting from yellow dominance.

**Value map strategy:** Yellow upper body reads against sky; black undercarriage grounds it to earth. The boom is the lightest element (catches top light); the tracks are the darkest (in ground shadow).
